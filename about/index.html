About me

I spent sixteen years in corporate strategy and ended up as a vice president with a corner office and a calendar I dreaded. I remember sitting in a quarterly review, presenting numbers I had worked a year for, and feeling nothing. Not sadness. Nothing. That was the day I started asking what my own next textbook was.

It took me two years to find it, and I made most of the mistakes my clients now avoid: I tried a bigger job, then a sabbatical, then a business school course, before I did the excavation work I now do with others. Coaching was the thing I had been doing in the corridors for years without calling it that.

Today I work one to one with a small number of leaders at a time, over six months, because real change in a settled career takes longer than a weekend retreat and less time than another decade of drifting.

The phrase I hear most often from clients by month three is some version of "I knew this already, I just stopped letting myself say it." That is the point. The answers were always there.
